Cyclone Design Optimization for Higher Efficiency and Lower Pressure Drop

Sized a Stairmand cyclone for a bagasse-fired boiler, then compared the standard, scroll-inlet and bell-mounted designs in CFD to see which gives the best balance of ash capture and fan power.

Case 0: standard inlet

Case 1: scroll inlet

Case 2: bell-mounted vortex finder

Key specifications
  • Stairmand high-efficiency cyclone, body diameter 1.0 m
  • Inlet 15 m/s at 160 °C (433.15 K), outlet −500 Pa gauge
  • Flue gas at 50% excess air: 72% N₂, 12.2% CO₂, 9.5% H₂O, 6.4% O₂
  • Ash density 1338.48 kg/m³, particles 1–17 µm, about 3,400 per size
  • Meshes of 1.3–1.5 million elements
Tools and methods

ANSYS Fluent (RNG k-ε, particle tracking), Stairmand and Lapple design equations, flue-gas density and viscosity calculations.

Results
DesignΔP (Pa)d₅₀ (µm)Total efficiency
Analytical5907.64n/a
Standard474.55.7695.0%
Scroll inlet418.78.2892%
Bell-mounted437.17.6793.6%

Scroll inlet cut pressure drop by 11.8% and bell-mounted by 7.9%, but both captured fewer fine particles. I recommended the bell-mounted design as the better compromise. The mesh was not fully independent, so I advised finer-mesh validation and a bag filter or ESP after the cyclone.

Community-scale solar-battery concept for rural electrification and clean water access, sized for 67 households.

Key specifications

Biomass Power Plant, 5.5 MW

Small-scale rice husk power plant based on the Rankine cycle, with component selection and economic analysis.

Key specifications
  • Steam flow 6.9 kg/s; boiler about 22.4 MW thermal (circulating fluidized bed)
  • Fuel use about 5.5 tons of rice husk an hour
  • Boiler, steam turbine, condenser, generator, cooling tower and pump, sized on Thailand climate data
Tools and methods

Rankine cycle calculations, component selection, efficiency analysis and techno-economic evaluation.

Results

5.5 MW net output at 24.7% plant efficiency. Capital cost 522 million THB, annual profit about 89 million THB, payback about 5.9 years.

Digital Twin HVAC Model

Digital twin of an inverter-controlled air conditioner that tests how a cleaner evaporator coil changes compressor power.

Key specifications

  • Output: compressor power in watts
Tools and methods

C# digital twin, MATLAB simulation and graphs, thermodynamic modeling of the HVAC cycle, heat transfer analysis of the evaporator.

Results

Compressor power fell from about 528 W to 483 W as the coil got cleaner, so a higher heat transfer coefficient saves energy.
